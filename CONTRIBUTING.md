# Contributing

Contributions are welcome after the repository is published. Until then, this document describes the intended process.

## Good contributions

- clearer source-governance rules;
- synthetic edge cases;
- improvements to evaluation criteria;
- accessibility and plain-language fixes;
- privacy and security safeguards;
- narrowly scoped workflow or template improvements; and
- validation tools that work on synthetic fixtures.

## Do not contribute

- real CVs, applications, messages, assessments, offers, or job-platform exports;
- personal contact or identity information;
- employer or client documents;
- credentials, tokens, cookies, or private URLs;
- copied proprietary prompts, policies, or datasets; or
- claims about a person that the person has not approved for publication.

## Contribution process

1. Open an issue that states the problem, scope, and expected benefit.
2. Keep the change small enough to review.
3. Use synthetic examples.
4. Update links and navigation when adding or moving content.
5. Run the quality, factual-grounding, privacy, and publishing checklists.
6. Explain any new data, dependency, automation, or security boundary.
7. Submit a pull request with a clear test or review record.

## Writing style

Use direct professional English. Define specialized terms, preserve uncertainty, avoid inflated claims, and link to the responsible central document rather than duplicating policy.

## Change control

A contribution does not become authoritative because it is polished or merged. Personal facts and contribution claims require the affected person's approval. Material architecture, privacy, automation, or external-action changes require explicit review.

Contributions are accepted under the repository's [MIT License](LICENSE). No separate Contributor License Agreement is currently required.
